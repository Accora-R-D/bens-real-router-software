    What gets machined

    Machines counterbores and blind pockets — recesses with a floor left in them, which need the part the right way up. Unticked, only features that go through the board are cut.

    Clears every pocket with a pocketing toolpath, so the waste leaves as chips rather than as a loose slug that can be picked up by the cutter. Untick it and only the pocket's internal profile is cut — faster, but the middle is left in.

    Cutting

    Dogbones relieve genuine sharp internal corners so mating parts seat. Modelled radii are left as drawn.

    Frame the nest first: before anything is cut, the tool traces the outline of the whole nest at the rapid height, so you can see the origin is on the stock and the rapids clear the clamps. Feed-hold if it is not.

    Finishing pass

    The roughing passes stop short of the wall and one last lap takes the skin off, so the edge is cut by a tool that is barely loaded and carries no step marks. Outer profiles and internal cut-outs only — pocket floors and area clearing are unchanged. The finished size is identical either way.

    Auto picks the widest loaded cutting tool that fits each feature. Force a seat only when you need to — a long-flute bit in a spare seat for holes the standard bit cannot reach through, say. A forced tool that does not fit a feature is swapped back, and the run log and job sheet say so.
